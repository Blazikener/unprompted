# Sell the license, not the receipt

**Mostly true: as Unprompted is framed today, both halves deliver their value once.** A creator's first Receipts scan surfaces years of on-camera brand mentions, and every later scan adds only that week's handful. Value that does not recur is the pattern behind churn even at Stan, the strongest $29/month creator comparable, whose leadership planned for gross retention in the 50s percent ([Creator-Entrepreneur](https://thecreatorentrepreneur.substack.com/p/stans-2023-shareholder-letter)). The brand email is a list of creators. Creator lists have been given away free since 2018, and finance teams discount "who mentioned us" as unverifiable earned media. The recurring, paid gap sits one step downstream: turning a creator's organic video into a licensed paid ad. **80% of influencers have been asked to let brands amplify their content, but only 51% charge for it** ([Lumanu](https://www.lumanu.com/blog/how-influencers-charge-whitelisting-usage-rights)). Agencies say usage rights are now priced as a separate add-on in nearly every deal, with no standard pricing ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/marketers-say-usage-rights-are-driving-up-the-price-to-work-with-creators/)). TikTok Spark codes die at the end of fixed 7- to 365-day windows, and brands keep lifting creators' unsponsored videos into ads without paying. That job repeats weekly for any brand that seeds and boosts creator content, and it ends in a cash transaction that both sides already know how to price: roughly 20-30% of a creator's base fee per month for paid usage. The catch is timing. ShopMy Spotlights (launched September 30, 2026), YouTube Brand Partner Access and TikTok's back-catalog licensing (announced September 2026) now run this exact flow, and they mostly pay creators commission or nothing upfront. That leaves Unprompted one opening: an upfront, pre-priced, consented license for untagged spoken mentions on Instagram and TikTok. It would be sold to brands, talent managers and, in the GCC, licensed agencies; creators would use it free, and Unprompted would earn a cut of each license. One caveat governs everything below. Reddit, Threads, Quora answers and X post bodies could not be read from this environment, so the evidence leans on Substack, trade press, review sites and vendor content, and no creator or brand was found stating an explicit price they would pay for this product.

## The scan and the list both deliver their value once

### Creators pay weekly for leads, not for proof of past mentions

The case that Receipts is a one-time tool for creators is strong. Its payoff lives in the back catalog: the first scan shows a creator which brands they have already praised on camera, and later scans can only add the few mentions from the latest week. Investors have long argued this is the weakest place to charge creators. Creator Ventures co-founder Sasha Kaletsky said creators buy tools that are about their content, not tools that make their business more efficient ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2023/09/26/creator-economy-startups-are-facing-a-judgment-day)). Founders who tested the idea directly got the same answer. One Hacker News founder messaged **600+ creators** to validate a brand-deal management tool, got no response at all, and concluded the problem was real "but not one painful enough for people to pay for" ([HN](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/items/46428771)). Sponsorgap, the closest analog found, served both creators seeking sponsors and brands seeking creators. It stalled around **$1,600 MRR** about a year after its first paying customer, and its founder's lesson was to pick one persona ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/sponsorgap)). Show HN launches of YouTube sponsor-finders drew one point each ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40130085); [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37544310)).

The buyer pool is squeezed from both ends. **68% of part-time creators earn under $1,000 a year**, and only **12% of full-timers clear $50K** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/20/linktree-creator-economy-report-research/embed/)). The creators who can afford $29/month often already have managers or steady inbound offers. Even Stan, which is the creator's cash register rather than a pitch-prep tool, saw year-over-year growth fall from **93% to 23.7%**. Sacra attributes the slowdown to churn on a single flat price tier catching up with new sign-ups ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/stan/)).

Yet pitching itself is a weekly habit for the creators who do it. Instagram creator and former talent manager Mia Macnair sends about **50 pitches a week**, gets 10-15% replies and lands 2-3 deals ([Substack](https://miamacnair.substack.com/p/i-send-50-brand-pitches-a-week-and)). A travel UGC creator reports that brands usually answer only on the third or fourth follow-up ([Travel Creator CEO](https://travelcreatorceo.substack.com/p/pitch-follow-up-email-templates-travel-ugc-creators)). Managers and coaches already tell creators to lead with brands they use ([Johanna Voss](https://johannavoss.substack.com/p/ive-closed-over-6m-in-brand-dealsheres)), so a list of past mentions mostly tells creators what they know.

What creators pay for, repeatedly, is knowing who is buying now. Macnair's weekly Creator Job Board costs **$5/month** ([Substack](https://miamacnair.substack.com/p/how-i-land-pr-gifting-and-ugc-deals)), and The Paid Creator's weekly leads newsletter reports 5,000+ subscribers ([Substack](https://thepaidcreator.substack.com/)). Brand PR email lists sell for about $10-20 on Gumroad ([Little Black Book](https://littleblackbook.gumroad.com/l/littleblackbook)), and a JoinBrands reviewer calls its **$48/month** tier worth the money ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/joinbrands.com)). The inbound success stories point the same way. A TikTok creator with under 6,000 followers got an unsolicited four-figure offer after a brand read her portfolio page, which led with an 8% engagement rate and reach of 23.8 times her follower count ([The Stack Report](https://thestackreportmag.substack.com/p/i-got-my-first-four-figure-brand)). A static scan is a receipt. A feed that pairs "you mentioned this brand four times" with "this brand is paying creators this week" is a lead, and leads are what creators already buy.

### Brands can already get creator lists free or bundled

The brand email faces a different problem: what it delivers is a commodity. Captiv8 made its database of 1M+ influencers free to brands in 2018 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/07/03/captiv8-creator-discovery/embed/)), and Modash now indexes **380M+ creator profiles** for 1,700+ brands ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49930681)). Upfluence and HypeAuditor already match a brand's own Shopify customers to creator profiles, HypeAuditor daily ([Upfluence](https://help.upfluence.co/en/articles/9083677-live-capture-quick-faq); [HypeAuditor](https://help.hypeauditor.com/en/articles/6961894-what-is-influential-customers)).

YouTube's FameBit is the cautionary data point for self-serve matching. Self-serve deals made up only **4% of creator payouts**, full-service deals paid creators **30 times more**, and YouTube shut self-serve down in 2020 ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2020/05/13/youtube-famebit-shutters-self-service-platform/)). A list of "creators who mentioned you" also reads to finance as earned media value (EMV). EMV has no standard formula: the same post can be valued at $12K or $120K depending on the multiplier, and it rarely ties to revenue ([ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/earned-media-value-emv-calculation/)). Even the vendor Aspire published an explainer asking whether EMV is meaningless ([Aspire](https://www.aspire.io/blog/is-emv-bs-rethinking-influencer-marketing-roi)).

UGC Roster's founder says brands now pay for a feedback loop showing which hooks and creators convert, not for exposure ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/brands-give-up-influencers-ugc-creator-economy)). Small brands also churn. In 2025 Sprout Social's net dollar retention (revenue kept from existing customers, including upsells) slipped to **100%**, against 102% when small-business customers are excluded, and the company is shifting its focus to customers paying $30K or more a year ([Sprout Social](https://investors.sproutsocial.com/news/news-details/2026/Sprout-Social-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-2025-Financial-Results/default.aspx)).

The weekly cadence, however, is right. Brands that seed products run weekly loops. One Shopify practitioner gifts about **100 creators a week**, sees roughly **5 of 100 post**, and converts the posters into affiliates. Programs like this are tracked in Google Sheets with hand-made Bitly links and discount codes ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/a-cautionary-look-at-influencer-program-management/570615)).

Merchants also keep paying for automated monitoring. Archive's Shopify listing shows 45 reviews averaging 5.0 that praise automatic capture of tagged posts and its use for listening and campaign tracking ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/archive-app-ugc-instagram-stories-tiktok/reviews?sort_by=newest)). The specific pain of missed spoken, untagged mentions, though, is voiced mainly by vendors, such as Syncly's unverified claim that about 97% of TikTok videos mentioning a brand don't tag it ([Syncly](https://syncly.app/blog/best-tiktok-creator-tool)). Independent reviewers complain about weak TikTok coverage in tools like Brandwatch ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/277011/Brandwatch/reviews/)), not about missed speech.

So "one-time" overstates the brand case. The weekly need is real, but a weekly list with no next action competes with free and bundled tools, and it loses.

## Licensing organic video is the weekly money event

The strongest recurring job in the evidence is turning creator videos, including unsponsored ones, into paid ads. Requests are routine. In a Lumanu and Collectively survey, 80% of influencers had been asked to let a partner amplify their content, but only 51% charged a fee, and smaller creators were the least likely to charge ([Lumanu](https://www.lumanu.com/blog/how-influencers-charge-whitelisting-usage-rights)). On the brand side, a 2026 CreatorIQ study (cited secondhand) found that **54% of paid-media managers and marketing executives** name securing usage rights as a top barrier to scaling creator content ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/perpetuity-whitelisting-ai-clauses-23-creator-economy-experts-on-what-brands-get-wrong-about-creator-usage-rights/)). Agency staff told Digiday that usage and exclusivity are now quoted as add-ons in virtually every deal, that pricing follows no consistent structure, and that usage scope is often undefined when the offer is made ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/marketers-say-usage-rights-are-driving-up-the-price-to-work-with-creators/)). Meta's partnership ads, which run a brand's ad from a creator's handle, are described both as the best-performing ad unit and as the hardest to scale ([Mike Shields](https://mikeshields.substack.com/p/meta-is-pushing-partnership-ads-in)).

The mechanics are what make this job recur. A TikTok Spark code authorizes one video for 7, 30, 60 or 365 days. Once it expires it cannot be reactivated, and the creator must issue a new one ([AdMakeAI](https://admakeai.com/blog/tiktok-spark-ads)). The same guide warns that a creator's personal music license does not cover commercial boosting. Agencies describe top-performing ads going dark mid-campaign because a 30-day code lapsed while the creator was away (search snippet; [HOC Paid Media](https://hocdigitalsolutions.co.uk/blog/tiktok-spark-ads-creator-content/)). Talent reps note that paid creative wears out within 4-6 weeks, which keeps usage windows short ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/perpetuity-whitelisting-ai-clauses-23-creator-economy-experts-on-what-brands-get-wrong-about-creator-usage-rights/)). A brand boosting a dozen creator videos therefore faces renewals, new requests and rights checks every week. That is the regular beat a weekly email needs. Archive already sells the request-a-Spark-code step as a product feature ([Archive Help](https://help.archive.com/en/articles/10199022-requesting-spark-ads-code)), which shows brands will pay for this workflow.

The same flow has a darker twin that sits squarely in Receipts' domain: brands lift organic videos into ads without asking. TheraBox reposted a creator's TikTok as a tagged Reel that drew 8,000+ views, for a product she had never used. Soft Services cut a still from an unsponsored video into a paid Instagram ad, then apologized but paid nothing ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/01/03/creators-content-stolen-by-brands-advertising-copyright/)). Refy compensated a creator only after she publicly called out an ad that had reached 114,900+ views ([Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/refy-beauty-ad-tiktok-2097/)). A teenager whose TikTok became a hookup-app ad is suing for **$750,000** in compensatory damages under false-endorsement and right-of-publicity claims ([FindLaw](https://www.findlaw.com/legalblogs/law-and-life/when-your-tiktok-becomes-an-ad-you-never-agreed-to-how-one-teen-is-fighting-back/)).

Creators are warned that brand comments asking to use their content usually mean perpetual, unpaid usage ([Jen Ruiz](https://jetplanecrew.substack.com/p/influencer-brand-deals-101)). Talent-manager guidance treats a brand reposting creator content it has not licensed as its own sales conversation ([UGC Roster](https://www.ugcroster.com/blog/how-to-pitch-brands-as-a-talent-manager)). An alert telling a creator that a brand has reused their mention would fire only occasionally, but each one could be worth real money, and that is the kind of event that brings users back. It also carries a warning. A brand email that surfaces creators without an easy, priced way to license their content could encourage this kind of lifting.

The table below ranks the recurring loops found in the evidence by how well they would sustain weekly use and payment.

| Recurring loop | Cadence | Who pays today, and roughly how much | Evidence strength | Fit with Unprompted's detection |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| License and renew organic clips as Spark or partnership ads | Weekly for brands that boost; 7-365 day code windows | Brands pay creators about 20-30% of base per month for paid usage, about 50% for allowlisting | Strong: surveys, trade press, creator rate cards | Direct: unprompted mentions are pre-qualified ad candidates |
| Seeding ROI: which gifted creators said our name | Weekly gifting batches | Brands pay capture and tracking tools (Archive estimated at ~$1,000/month) | Moderate: one practitioner thread plus reviews | Direct: spoken mentions catch untagged posts |
| Warm-lead feed: brands you mention that are paying creators now | Weekly pitching | Creators pay $5-48/month for lead lists and job access | Strong revealed preference | Partial: needs a "who is hiring" signal added |
| Roster scan for talent managers | Monday pipeline review, monthly per-creator report | Managers pay ~$29 per creator or $99-119 per 25 creators | Moderate: mostly vendor sources | Direct: no tool found that tracks unpaid mentions across a roster |
| Detecting a brand's reuse of a creator's clip | Sporadic | Creators recover money only after public callouts | Moderate: news cases | Direct |
| Payment chasing and proof at invoice time | Per deal | Creators absorb the cost | Strong pain (48% of paid creators paid late) | Weak: outside the core |

The payment row matters because it is the most widespread pain, not because Receipts should own it. 48% of 500+ paid creators surveyed were paid late ([Lumanu](https://www.lumanu.com/blog/insights-from-500-influencers-on-their-payment-experience)), and creators describe Net 60 to Net 120 terms as routine ([Aakanksha Monga](https://aakankshamonga.substack.com/p/exposing-brand-red-flags)). A licensing product that collects payment upfront and releases it when the code is delivered would solve this pain for the licenses it brokers, at no extra cost to build.

## ShopMy, YouTube and TikTok entered this flow within two years

The licensing gap is real, which is why platforms are closing it. ShopMy launched Spotlights on September 30, 2026. It surfaces creator content that is already driving affiliate sales so brands can run it as Meta partnership ads or TikTok Spark Ads. Creators can switch on Instant Spotlights to pre-approve requests from brands they mention, which is close to the Receipts premise ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/09/30/shop-my-spotlight-creator-content-becomes-campaign-ads/); [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/shopmy-launches-spotlights-tool-to-turn-organic-creator-posts-into-paid-ads/)). Creators are paid standard commission rather than upfront fees. ShopMy cites a case study with the brand MUTHA showing **+55% ROAS and +140% GMV**, across a network of 350,000 creators.

YouTube Brand Partner Access lets brands request organic videos, and creators can auto-accept requests from a given advertiser, but YouTube stays out of payment ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15672082)). Since November 2024, creators with 4,000+ subscribers can also send their Shorts to brands with licensing rights attached and no direct payment ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/11/06/youtube-shorts-creators-send-videos-to-brands/)).

At its September 2026 Creators Summit, TikTok announced two things. Creators can earn from letting brands retroactively run their organic videos as ads, and TikTok One Pay will pay brand deals within 30 days at zero commission. TikTok published no rates ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/09/29/tiktok-one-pay-creator-brand-deal-compensation/)). TikTok's Content Suite reportedly already finds organic brand mentions through speech recognition, though TikTok's own documentation was not checked ([BrandMentions](https://brandmentions.com/blog/track-tiktok-mentions/)).

| Player | What overlaps with Unprompted | How creators get paid | Gap Unprompted can still claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| ShopMy Spotlights (Sep 2026) | Organic posts become Spark or partnership ads; creators pre-approve brands they mention | Affiliate commission only | Mentions with no affiliate history; upfront license fee |
| YouTube Brand Partner Access and Shorts linking | Brands request organic videos; creators can auto-accept | YouTube is not involved; linking pays nothing | YouTube only; payment and terms left to the parties |
| TikTok back-catalog licensing and One Pay (Sep 2026) | Organic videos run as ads after the fact | Rates unpublished | Instagram; one view across platforms |
| Archive | Watches and listens for untagged mentions; Spark-code requests | Not a creator payment tool | Price (~$1,000/month estimated); small merchants objected to tier changes |
| Syncly | Transcribes TikTok audio; turns hidden advocates into affiliates | Affiliate commission | No buyer reviews found; sells affiliates, not licenses |
| SponsorTrace, ThoughtLeaders, MeetSponsors, SponsorRadar, GetSponsored | Which brands pay which creators; matches brands to a roster | Subscriptions of $39-119/month | YouTube-centric; tracks declared sponsorships, not unpaid mentions |
| Agentio, Passionfroot, Collabstr | Paid deal flow, with a cut | 15-20% of deal or media spend | New deals rather than licensing existing organic videos |

Archive says its AI watches video, listens to audio and captures 400% more content than competing platforms ([Archive](https://archive.com/blog/ugc-platforms-mobile-apps)). A third-party review estimates its price at around $1,000 a month ([KaloIQ](https://www.kaloiq.com/reviews/archive/)). One merchant cut its rating to two stars after Archive removed its free tier, objecting to tiers that scale with store sessions ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/archive-app-ugc-instagram-stories-tiktok/reviews?sort_by=newest)).

Syncly claims 3-4 times more brand mentions than text-first tools and names LG, Tiffany, Adidas and Samsung as customers ([Syncly](https://syncly.app/social/solution/brand-monitor)).

Sponsor databases are crowded and centered on YouTube. SponsorTrace charges $39-49 a month for creators and **$99-119 a month for agencies covering up to 25 creators**, and it detects new YouTube deals daily ([SponsorTrace](https://sponsortrace.com/solutions/talent-agencies)). GetSponsored has three Trustpilot reviews, and none reports landing a sponsorship ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/getsponsored.io)).

Deal marketplaces make money from transactions. Agentio reportedly keeps about 20% of the media spend it handles (search snippet; [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/agentio)), and it cut the time to a creator's first bid from 45-50 days to under one day ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/18/agentio-secures-40m-from-forerunner-as-it-scales-its-creator-marketplace-beyond-youtube/)). Passionfroot takes 5% of deals creators bring and 15% of deals from its network ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/21/passionfroot-is-building-a-platform-for-brand-and-creator-colloboration-with-a-focus-on-b2b-sector)), and its revenue grew 13 times in a year ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/22/passionfroot-raises-15m-to-expand-its-b2b-creator-marketplace-to-the-us/)).

The category is also unstable. TikTok shut its standalone Creator Marketplace in April 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/27/tiktok-sunsets-its-creator-marketplace-for-tiktok-one-a-broader-solution-with-ai-tools)). Flagship, backed by Sequoia, closed its creator storefront and pivoted to a creator search engine ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/flagship-shuts-down-e-commerce-platform-pivots-to-creator-search-engine/)).

Four positions remain open:

- **Paid, upfront licenses.** The platform defaults price a creator's license at commission or zero, so an upfront, pre-priced license that the creator explicitly approves is unclaimed. Creators' anger at unpaid reuse shows they want it, but it has to compete with free alternatives.
- **Cross-platform coverage.** Each platform tool covers only its own platform, while Unprompted reads Instagram and TikTok transcripts together.
- **Roster-level monitoring.** No tool found tracks a talent roster's unpaid mentions.
- **Arabic.** Detecting mentions in Arabic is untested by anyone.

The creator pattern is consistent. **About $29/month is the credible ceiling, and only when the tool is visibly tied to income.** Creators resent roughly $30/month when the benefit is cosmetic, they pay $5-48 for leads, and they accept 5-15% cuts of deal value. Stan expected about half its payers to churn within a year even though it is the creators' cash register ([Creator-Entrepreneur](https://thecreatorentrepreneur.substack.com/p/stans-2023-shareholder-letter)). A pitch-prep subscription should expect to churn faster.

Brands under roughly $5-10M in revenue are price-sensitive; one GRIN reviewer said that was the scale at which the tool would make sense ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/173654/GRIN/)). Brands are furious about lock-ins. One GRIN reviewer described auto-payments that could not be stopped. Aspire allegedly refused a cancellation and kept charging $500 a month. Upfluence allegedly told a customer at cancellation that a 6-month contract was actually 12 ([Capterra GRIN](https://www.capterra.com/p/173654/GRIN/reviews/); [Capterra Aspire](https://www.capterra.com/p/187445/AspireIQ/reviews/); [Capterra Upfluence](https://www.capterra.com/p/162056/Upfluence/reviews/)). Month-to-month billing is therefore a selling point in its own right.

Talent managers apply a simple payback rule: a tool should cost less than one deal's commission and produce at least one extra deal a month ([UGC Roster](https://www.ugcroster.com/blog/free-talent-manager-software)). At 20% of a $2K-10K deal, a manager earns $400-2,000. A $99-299/month roster seat therefore pays for itself with roughly one extra mid-size deal a quarter. That is an illustrative calculation, not an observed purchase.

The take-rate arithmetic is the most important caution in this section. At the creator-stated 20-30% of base per month, a micro creator with a $500 base earns $100-150 per 30-day license, and a 15% cut leaves Unprompted $15-23 per license-month. **Matching the revenue of one $299/month brand seat takes 13-20 active licenses.** A pure take rate works only at volume or with larger creators. A hybrid is safer: a modest brand subscription for the weekly feed plus a cut of each license. There is also a larger base than the creator fee. Usage fees are sometimes set at 3-10% of the paid media spend behind the content (search snippet; [Digiday](https://digiday.com/future-of-tv/future-of-tv-briefing-brands-are-spending-more-to-advertise-creators-content-making-usage-rights-a-focal-point/)), and Agentio's model shows a percentage of media spend scales far beyond a percentage of creator fees.

## Counter-evidence: affinity converts unevenly and platforms anchor at zero

The core premise, that brands want creators who already love them, holds up, but conversion does not follow automatically. Youthforia's VP of marketing called influencer conversion results mixed, and the agency Square Root Creative found little to no return in sales or web traffic ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/dtc-briefing-as-influencer-marketing-gets-more-expensive-brands-test-out-new-approaches/)). Jones Road Beauty's CEO says roughly 3 in 10 creators perform, and that bigger influencers outperform micro-creators in paid ads ([Superfiliate](https://www.superfiliate.com/field-notes/where-ugc-influencer-and-paid-media-collide-with-cody-plofker-ceo-of-jones-road-beauty)). That cuts directly against licensing small creators' organic clips as performance ads. GRIN reviewers add that creators with strong stats often underperform ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/173654/GRIN/reviews/)).

The other side has advocates too. Maesa's CMO argues organic content beats paid advertising ([Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/brands-are-betting-big-on-organic-content/)). A brand co-founder says creators post about products that help them get views whether they are paid or not ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/dtc-briefing-as-influencer-marketing-gets-more-expensive-brands-test-out-new-approaches/)). Still, no verified 2024-2026 case was found of a nano or micro creator closing a paid deal by showing a brand their unprompted mention. The one documented organic-to-paid path took about three months of product trades before the first paid deal ([FoundHer Files](https://foundherfiles.substack.com/p/why-most-paid-partnerships-start)).

Creators are also less inclined to charge than the licensing thesis assumes. Smaller creators often grant amplification free because they value the reach ([Lumanu](https://www.lumanu.com/blog/how-influencers-charge-whitelisting-usage-rights)). The platforms anchor brands at zero or commission-only pricing. Telling a brand about an unpaid mention can invite it to keep reposting for free, a risk talent-agency founder Jessica Mann's account of brands sharing creator information with each other makes plausible ([Jessica Mann](https://jessicajanemann.substack.com/p/why-talent-managers-keep-losing-to)).

Flagship closed its creator storefront because creators preferred making content to running a store and resisted promoting brands they didn't know ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/flagship-shuts-down-e-commerce-platform-pivots-to-creator-search-engine/)). That supports the "brands you already mention" angle, but it also means the creator side must take almost no effort. Creators with steady inbound offers don't pitch at all. Macnair said she had stopped pitching after optimizing her profile ([Substack](https://miamacnair.substack.com/p/how-i-land-pr-gifting-and-ugc-deals)), and a creator strategist who says she has worked with 600+ brands argues brand deals are not passive income and are a poor fit for most mom creators ([Monica Banks](https://momcreators.substack.com/p/the-truth-about-brand-deals)). Trade press also reports brands moving away from mass "surprise and delight" gifting toward opt-in seeding ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2024/03/04/why-surprise-and-delight-doesn-t-apply-to-influencer-gifting)). That shrinks seeding volume, though it raises the value of knowing in advance who already talks about the product.

Some evidence also cuts against the pessimistic reading of Unprompted. Creators do pay for subscriptions tied to growth or cash. The X growth tool SuperX reportedly reached about $23K MRR from roughly 650 payers at $39/month (search snippet; [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/hitting-23k-mrr-in-six-months-after-five-failures-4d64o9ev4AXXhX9ogHQQ)), and Stan reached about $40M ARR at $29/month ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/stan/)). Monitoring tools can also retain brands. Archive's merchants stay, and the SMB listening tool Brand24 reported churn of about 5%, though the period is unstated ([Valueships](https://www.valueships.com/case-studies/case-brand24)). The lesson is not that creators and brands refuse to pay. It is that they pay for tools that sit on the path money takes, and Receipts as a standalone scan does not.

## In the GCC, licensing rules make agencies the buyer

GCC discussion of influencer marketing is dominated by regulation, and regulation places agencies at the center. In the UAE, talent agencies representing influencers must be accredited by the Media Council. Permits for non-resident creators are filed through accredited agencies. Legal guides list four accredited agencies as of early 2026: Xpozed, New Media Academy, Imfluence and The Agency 971. These details come from search snippets and should be checked against the Council's own site ([DIAC](https://diac.ae/blog/influencer-license-dubai-uae/)).

Saudi Arabia's Mawthooq license costs **SAR 15,000 for three years**, carries fines of up to SAR 5M, and reportedly shrank the active commercial influencer base by about 35%. That figure is a directional estimate from Kolsquare, and the same source describes license checks as a filter on which creators get cast ([Everything-PR](https://everything-pr.com/the-rise-of-influencer-marketing-in-saudi-arabia-opportunities-challenges-and-key-players)). The rules extend to visiting influencers ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-tightens-rules-on-influencer-ads-with-mandatory-licensing-1.1733131320843)).

The economics show a market where unpaid and barter work is still common. Yamammi, a Dubai agency (a vendor source), reviewed 350 agreements for 100 clients. It found creator fees made up 40-60% of all-in campaign budgets, and six creators signed **AED 0 contracts** just for access ([Yamammi](https://yamammi.com/receipts/dubai-influencer-rate-report)). Vendor rate guides say Dubai rates run 20-30% above London and New York, which they attribute to licensing costs, and that pure barter now works only for products worth more than AED 1,000 (search snippets; [Homestead](https://homestead-ae.com/uae-influencer-rates-salary-guide/); [Influencer.vip](https://influencer.vip/blogs/nano-influencers-dubai-price-2026-guide-to-rates-and-roi)). The UAE tax authority has warned that free gifts and experiences are taxable ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/uae-tax-authority-warns-influencers-that-free-gifts-and-experiences-are-taxable-1.1180225)).

On the brand side, a 2023 Campaign Middle East piece reported that **55% of UAE in-house marketers struggle to find relevant influencers**, though it did not name the original survey ([Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/the-rise-of-the-influencer-agency/)). Dubai agencies sell shortlists with a rationale for each creator, audience data and indicative cost, plus reports on earned media and share of voice ([Creators Dubai](https://creators-dubai.com/)).

For Unprompted, this points to a GCC buyer that is a licensed agency or talent manager running a roster, not a solo creator. The product would need to filter shortlists to licensed creators and feed the earned-media reports agencies already bill for.

Two possible differentiators are plausible but unproven. The first is detecting spoken mentions in Arabic and in Arabic-English code-switching. No source showed demand for it or any competitor offering it. SMB listening tools draw complaints about weak non-English accuracy (search snippet; [Capterra Brand24](https://www.capterra.com/p/149054/Brand24/reviews/)). Whether Oriane's transcripts handle Gulf Arabic is itself untested. The second is a compliance angle. Mawthooq and the UAE's permit rules separate paid promotion from organic content, so an unpaid on-camera mention may double as a compliance flag: is this promotion, and is it licensed and disclosed? That needs legal review before anyone sells it.

The research found no first-person GCC practitioner accounts of tools or workflow, and no regional commission norms, so any GCC plan starts as a discovery exercise with a handful of accredited agencies.

## Build a priced license request first, then a roster seat

The evidence supports rebuilding the brand email around one action per row. Each unprompted mention would carry its views, its engagement against a benchmark, a brand-safety check, a music-license risk flag and the creator's licensing status. Beside it would sit a one-click request for a 30-day license at a price set from creator norms (20-30% of an implied base per month, with a floor). The creator receives the request in Receipts and approves it by issuing a Spark code or authorizing a partnership ad. Unprompted collects payment upfront, keeps 10-15%, and tracks expiry so the following week's email can say which licenses are about to lapse and which new mentions arrived.

That turns the email from a list into the regular beat of a transaction. It also gives creators a reason to keep Receipts installed without paying for it. Their weekly events become license requests, alerts that a brand reused a clip without a license, and, as a later addition, a feed of brands they have mentioned that are paying creators this week. The first vertical should be beauty and fashion, because those brands already paid Tribe Dynamics to measure organic mentions before CreatorIQ acquired it ([Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/creatoriq-acquire-influencer-marketing-analytics-platform-tribe-dynamics/1728053)).

The second product is a roster seat for talent managers and agencies at $99-299/month. It would deliver a Monday list of brands each creator already mentions, a monthly "brand mentions" section for the per-creator report managers already produce, and detection of brands reposting roster content without a deal. Brands explicitly ask managers to bring data in their pitches ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kahleawade_brands-that-partner-with-talent-managers-activity-7299888013709295616-b0t3)), and no tool found covers a roster's organic mentions. Three things should not be built: a deal-management CRM (the 600-creator test), an open two-sided marketplace before one side pays (Sponsorgap, FameBit), and another YouTube sponsor database.

The thresholds below are proposed decision rules for the founder, not research findings.

| Test | Hypothesis | Method | Proposed pass signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand license concierge | Brands will pay upfront to license unprompted clips | 20 beauty, fashion or DTC brands with 3+ unprompted mentions each get a hand-built weekly email for 4 weeks, with pre-priced Stripe links per clip | 3+ brands buy at least one license; at least one renews |
| Creator acceptance and price | Creators accept pre-priced 30-day licenses at 20-30% of implied base | Contact the creator behind every brand request; log acceptance, time to Spark code and counteroffers | 50%+ accept within 72 hours |
| Receipts packaging | Free with license payouts retains better than a $29 scan | Three-arm landing test: $29 Pro scan, free plus payouts, low-priced weekly warm-lead feed anchored to the $5-48 lead market | Week-4 active rate and paid conversion by arm |
| Seeding ROI | Spoken detection finds materially more posting creators than tags and codes | 5 Shopify brands upload gifted-order lists; compare spoken mentions against tagged and code-based counts | At least 2x more posters found (vendors claim 3-4x) |
| Talent-manager roster | Roster scans produce pitches that close | 10 managers with 5-25 creators get weekly scans for 6 weeks | 1+ closed deal across the cohort; 3+ managers commit at $99 or more |
| GCC agency pilot | Accredited agencies pay for Arabic-aware roster scans | 3-5 UAE or KSA licensed agencies; measure precision on Arabic and code-switched content | Acceptable precision (for example 80%+) and one paid pilot |
| Unlicensed-reuse alert | Brand reuse is frequent enough to drive creator retention | Sample creators' mentions; check brand accounts and ad libraries for reposts | Frequency high enough to fire monthly for active creators |

Sequencing matters more than any single feature. The brand-license and creator-acceptance tests can run by hand in parallel within four weeks. Together they answer the only question that justifies the rest: will money move through Unprompted every week?

## Reddit was unreachable, so vendor voices are overweighted

Reddit could not be fetched at all. The old.reddit.com, www.reddit.com and JSON search endpoints failed, and site-restricted web searches surfaced no usable threads. That removes the communities where creators, UGC sellers and DTC marketers most often post first-person rants, rate disclosures and tool keep-or-cancel lists (r/InfluencerMarketing, r/UGCcreators, r/TikTokCreators, r/ecommerce). Other gaps compound it:

- **Blocked or unreadable sources:** Threads and Quora answer pages returned errors, X post bodies were blocked, TikTok discover pages did not render, G2 returned 403, and LinkedIn posts were mostly not indexed.
- **No practitioner forum:** Hacker News has almost no discussion from practitioners in this space.
- **What the evidence leans on instead:** creator Substacks, several written by people who also sell pitching products or lists; trade press quoting named operators; Trustpilot, Capterra and Shopify reviews, which skew toward support-prompted praise or grievances; and a large share of vendor content from UGC Roster, Lumanu, Archive, Syncly, Modash and CreatorIQ.
- **Weak spots in the data:**
  - Some survey years are unclear, including Lumanu's late-payment and amplification surveys.
  - Some claims rest on search snippets rather than fetched pages, and are flagged where they appear.
  - Quotes passed through a page-summarizing tool and should be spot-checked before external use.
  - No source reported churn or weekly-active rates for the closest comparables.
  - There are no first-person GCC practitioner voices and no Arabic-language sources.
  - No buyer reviews of Syncly were found.

The direction of the findings is consistent across source types, but the specific thresholds and price points should be treated as anchors to test, not as measured demand.

## Conclusion

The question the founder asked, whether this is one-time use, turns out to be the wrong unit of analysis. The useful question is where money changes hands every week. In this market that happens at the moment an organic video becomes a paid ad, and the 7- to 365-day expiry on those licenses gives the market a built-in weekly rhythm. Unprompted's real asset is not detection, which TikTok, Archive and Syncly increasingly offer. It is that the mentions it finds are authentic endorsements, made without payment or prompting. That asset becomes valuable only when it turns into a priced, consented license. A consented license is also the ethical alternative to the reposting cases that keep producing callouts and lawsuits.

Two implications follow. First, the window is short. ShopMy and TikTok moved into this flow in the last week of September 2026, both anchoring creators at commission or undisclosed rates, so a founder who proves brands will pay upfront for cross-platform licenses has months, not years, to establish that position. Second, the arithmetic of small licenses points away from solo nano creators as the paying customer and toward the people who aggregate them: brands boosting many clips, talent managers running rosters, and in the GCC the licensed agencies that regulation already makes the gatekeepers. Receipts survives as the free front door for creators, and the revenue comes from brands, managers and agencies.
